# mies

## What it does

`mies` is the space designer: form, plans and structure. It reads the brief, the project's `DESIGN.md` and the project's own rules, then hands back a design study: a model of a building or a space as a few named solid bodies, or a plan, a section or a sheet in metric units, with its figures and an image.

Its file also holds a short stance: form from the task, the most effect for the least means, and no form chosen for its look. A stance is not a voice.

It calls four tools, each on a stated fact: `design` when no brief file exists, `massing` when the task is the form or volume of a building or space, `plans` when the task is a floor plan, a section or a sheet, and `critique` when a finished piece exists that this session did not make. The default is not to call.

The job is space, not page layout. Page grids, type and screens stay with `design`, `brand` and `screens`, so there is no second owner of a grid. A structural grid on a plan is a layout of columns and spans, and it is not the grid of a token file.

Every hand-over says: this is a design study, not an engineered design. It has no structural calculation and no code check. It also says what the study does not have: floors, stairs, openings or structure, unless the script made them.

The split is fixed. The writer owns the words of the pieces made here, donna keeps long documents and the PDF build, `bayer` takes brand, logos and email, `breuer` takes app and web screens, and louis reviews.

## When to reach for it

- A building or a space needs its form and volume looked at before anything is drawn.
- A floor plan, a section or a sheet is wanted, with its dimensions and a note on its structure and material.
- A finished piece from another session needs a craft pass.

Skip it for brand, logos, email, app and web screens and document text.

## Common questions

**Is the result an engineered design?** No. It has no structural calculation and no code check, and the hand-over says so.

**What does a hand-over say about the volume?** It gives the volume as the sum of the bodies, so any overlap is counted twice, and it lists the overlaps found.

**Does it mark its own work?** No. The craft pass runs on a piece another session made, and it is not a sign-off.

**Is this a person speaking?** No. The register is plain and professional, and nothing in the file imitates a real person's voice or attributes words to anyone.

## It's working if

- Every hand-over carries the design-study line and says what the study does not have.
- Every figure in a hand-over equals what the app printed, with its unit.
- Every body is closed and named for what it is, and every plan has its layers, its unit and its dimensions.
- It calls a tool only for the stated trigger, and says in one line which tool and why.
