# Email registers

The look of a category, as a short list of rules read from real marketing emails. Each register was written from pictures of finished emails that were opened and looked at on the date given. Long emails were looked at as reduced contact sheets, so small print was not read in all of them.

A register is a starting point, not a template. The brief names the register it used and what it departs from, and the piece's own research (`research.md`, step P3 in the `design` tool) adds what its sources show. A register ranks as an optional reference: the brief, `DESIGN.md` and the project's own rules outrank it.

These registers describe other makers' work in our words. No picture is copied here, and brand copyright stays with the brands. A number in brackets points at the source with that number in the register's own list.

Each register carries a date and a review-by date. `npm run check-skills` warns when one is overdue, and does not fail.

## Sports
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- One column of about 600 px, image first: a full-bleed hero of an athlete, bike or shoe above one headline, one short paragraph and one button (1, 2, 3).
- Below it, product in 2-up or 3-up grids, or in alternating image and colour-tile rows (1, 3, 4).
- Claim rows beside a photo carry the argument: a figure and a label next to test imagery (2, 3).
- A media newsletter runs headline module, then small modules with coloured labels, then a sponsor-logo wall (6).
- Footers are dark and brand-heavy: an oversize wordmark, app badges, a trust row (5).

### Imagery
Action and fabric detail in tight crops, studio product on black with a low rim light, product beside sculpted 3D lettering, styled flat-lays with props, athletes on court in location light, race and portrait documentary. People are rarely smiling; the product or the effort is the subject.

### Type
Wide or neutral grotesque headlines, often in capitals (2, 3, 6). Mono for captions and product labels (1). Serif body with script place-name logotypes (4). Body copy is small, about 11 to 13 px, and short.

### Colour
Mostly black, white and one brand accent: black and red (2, 6), dark green with neon green product (5), cream with wine and gold (4), mint and black (3). Photography supplies the rest.

### Story
Proof or place: a performance claim, a named drop, a race result or a first-look label. Subject lines lead with the product name or the event.

### CTA
One solid dark rectangle or pill per module, repeated down the email. Secondary options are plain text links or a second button beside the first (4). No discount badges except a free-shipping strip (3).

## Tech
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- Hero first, one claim, one button. Dark emails centre a single product render (1, 2); one maker wraps everything in a dark rounded card on a white page (3).
- Proof comes as short icon rows or an outlined spec box (1, 2).
- Two other shapes: the product-update letter, a long single column of plain text with screenshots, quotes and numbered steps (4, 5), and the playful image and text zigzag with small links (6).
- Footers are quiet: social icons, small grey legal text, footnote numbers for claims.

### Imagery
Studio renders and macro on dark with dramatic light (1, 2). Product on colour with a model (6). One lifestyle photo of the product in use (5). Product screenshots in place of photos for software (3, 4).

### Type
Mono capitals for headlines (1), a display serif over a sans body (2), the maker's own clean sans (3, 4), a large light grotesque (5), heavy white sans on a colour field (6). One or two weights per email.

### Colour
Near-black with white type is the default. One accent per brand: blue (4), pink (6), a blue-purple gradient (3). Light mode appears only in the letter-style emails.

### Story
A one-line claim, or an announcement addressed to a named audience (4). Offers sit at the bottom as a strip, such as a trade-in or included apps (1, 3).

### CTA
A white pill on dark, an outline button on dark (1), a solid dark pill on white (5), a small link with an arrow (6). One primary button per hero, repeated once or twice. Software emails use inline underlined links in place of buttons.

## Fashion
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- A centred or left masthead, then one large image, a headline, two lines of copy and one button. Everything else is image.
- Grids of cut-out product on pale grey squares, 3-up (3) or 2-up with a label under each (4).
- Luxury houses stop after two or three images and a footer list (1, 2). Mid-market brands go on to a grid and a feature card (4, 5).
- Story emails run long: a narrow column, image beside text, dated paragraphs, archive documents (6).
- Footer: a small link list, social icons, tiny legal text.

### Imagery
Campaign editorial with a single model, often cropped or looking away (1, 2, 3). Detail flat-lays of fabric and accessories. Studio cut-outs on grey for product (3, 4, 5). Campus and location lifestyle (4). Archive imagery and a press letter (6).

### Type
The logotype does the branding. Headlines are small and plain, 14 to 20 px, with capitals for labels (3). Body is 10 to 12 px. Serif only for editorial titles (5); typewriter mono for a narrative (6).

### Colour
Black, white and neutral; the garment is the colour. A brand tint at most: warm taupe (4), slate and cream (5), red and navy (6).

### Story
The drop is the hook: a named collection or season. Where there is a story it is a fact about the object (6).

### CTA
A small black rectangle with a capitals label, or an underlined capitals text link (3). A paired choice for her and for him (1). Rarely more than one CTA per image block. No discount banners except a free-shipping strip (5).

### Story

### CTA
Rounded pill buttons in the brand's accent (1, 2, 4), a small rectangle with an arrow (3), a block (5). A button per section, with a short imperative label in capitals. Sign-up and rewards are secondary calls.

## Food and drink
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- A hero with an oversized stacked headline, often over the photograph (1), or a flat colour hero with the product centred (4, 5, 7).
- Then product tiles: 2-up image and name rows (1), one large product with a text block (2), an icon and caption trio (3), or two features stacked, each image in a white circle (6).
- Dividers are shaped: scalloped or wavy edges between colour bands (6), rounded photo frames (3, 4), a sticker-style card (4).
- Footer: social icons, an app or rewards strip (1), a plain address.

### Imagery
Appetite first: hand-held food at an outdoor table in natural light (3), a drink stack in warm side light (1), a burger shot straight on with every layer visible (6), cookies beside a prop (4). Drink brands use packshots on a flat or gradient ground (5, 7) or low-key product on grey with long shadows (2). People appear once, laughing around a table (7).

### Type
Heavy display sans with tight leading, in capitals for headlines (1, 7), a high-contrast serif with a rounded sans for a craft feel (3), monospace for a specialist feel (2), a blackletter wordmark for a loud feel (5). Short lines; one voice, usually witty.

### Colour
The brand's own flat colour fills the email: green and cream (1, 3), black and gold (5), yellow and beige (6), cyan and navy (7), near-black with orange (4), neutral grey (2). A seasonal accent rides on top.

### Story
Season or occasion: a menu change, a holiday, gift season. Limited edition or back for a limited time is the usual lever. Humour in the first line (5, 7).

### CTA
Pill or block buttons in the brand colour with short, loud labels. The first CTA is above the fold; the same action repeats under each feature.

## Finance and fintech
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- A single column that is mostly text: a brand mark, one headline, one paragraph, one button, then proof as cards or a table.
- A comparison table inside the email (1). 2 by 2 benefit cards with icons (4). Two benefit icons on a dark blue band (6). Feature modules each pairing a headline with a phone screenshot (2).
- A newsletter layout with section labels, a chart, a byline and a reading list (5). A letter layout: greeting, one screenshot, three bullets, one button (3).
- Legal text is long and unavoidable: footnotes under features (2), a terms page of grey type at the end (1, 6). Treat it as part of the design.

### Imagery
Almost none. Illustration (1), 3D or stylised card art (2, 6), UI screenshots (2, 3), stock objects such as coins (4), a flat illustration in the newsletter (5). People appear only in the app screens.

### Type
Clean grotesque or humanist sans; a display serif for the most confident headline (4) and a soft rounded sans for the friendliest (1, 2). The number is the typographic hero (1, 5, 6).

### Colour
An off-white or tinted ground with one brand colour block: mint and forest green (1), warm beige and brown (2), purple-mauve (4), royal and navy blue (6), light blue (3), muted purple on cream (5). Trust colours dominate for incumbents; warmer palettes for challengers.

### Story
A number or a promise: bonus points, a fee-free account, a saving, a rate. Challenger brands wrap it in a belief or a feature drop. Onboarding emails say how little time it takes (1).

### CTA
One solid rounded button in the brand's dark colour whose label is an action on the account. It appears after the hero and again after the last feature. Disclosures are underlined links, never buttons.

### Layout
- A hero, a short line and one button; then one of three bodies: a 2 by 2 category grid of rounded photos with underlined labels (1), a benefits grid plus a product row with prices (2), or a product spotlight followed by a row of variants (4).
- An announcement strip at the top carries the code or shipping line (1); a trust strip near the bottom carries shipping, returns, instalments and a store locator (4, 5).
- An editorial gift guide: numbered tips, a quote, 2-up product tiles on pale squares, a promo card (3).
- A collection launch with a sale band: an offset hero photo, 2-up product cards with captions, a solid band for the sale (6).

### Imagery
Lifestyle interiors in warm, low light for home goods (1, 6). Product cut-outs on a flat brand colour (4, 5). A product with a prop for a visual joke (4). An illustrated hero for programme or membership emails (2). People appear in editorial emails (3).

### Type
Capitals display for the hero, a serif or italic serif for warmth (5), a neutral grotesque for modern (4, 6). Prices and codes are plain bold. Body copy is 14 px, centred or left.

### Colour
One brand colour floods a block: red (4), terracotta, cream and green (5), olive (6), teal (2), navy and warm neutral (1). Product colour is the headline when it is the news (4).

### Story
Product news with scarcity: back in stock, just landed, now in a new finish. Welcome emails offer a first-order discount with the code printed as text (1) or explain membership benefits in four tiles (2).

### CTA
A solid block or pill in the brand colour whose label names the product. Repeated after each section. Text links for secondary routes.

## Travel
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

### Layout
- Photograph first. Then the repeated card: a place photo, a small label, a title, a days-and-price line or a rating, a button (1, 2, 5, 6).
- An offer panel above the cards when there is a promotion: two numbers side by side, one button (5).
- Brand-wall stacks: full-width hotel banners with the city name set over the photo (3).
- A dark, big-type app promotion with a features list of small thumbnails (4).
- Footer: app badges, a contact line, accreditation logos (2), terms in small print.

### Imagery
Place over people. Wide, saturated landscape or architecture at golden hour (1, 2, 5, 6). Interiors with a single object of mood (3). Forest and landscape for outdoor apps (4). Pairs of crops, a detail beside a wide, for hotels (5). People appear as a small icon grid for services (1).

### Type
Clean sans for utility (1, 6); serif headlines for heritage and luxury (2, 5); bold rounded display for outdoors (4); a wordmark as the headline over photos (3). Titles are short; descriptions run two to four lines.

### Colour
Brand-led: navy and a teal button (5), black with an orange button (3), dark green with a lime button (4), bright blue (6), white with a black button (1), navy with red illustration (2). Photography supplies warm colour against a dark or flat ground.

### Story
Destination or price: a named place, a points offer, a percentage, a price drop with the struck-through price (6), or a member rate (3). A question headline gives curiosity.

### CTA
One saturated button with the action in the label. Cards use quieter outline buttons. App badges close the email.
