# Brand registers

The look of a brand identity sheet, as a short list of rules read from real brand manuals. The register was written from pages that were opened and looked at on the date given.

A register is a starting point, not a template. The brief names the register it used and what it departs from, and the piece's own research (`research.md`, step P3 in the `design` tool) adds what its sources show. A register ranks as an optional reference: the project's own rules, the brief and `DESIGN.md` outrank it.

This register describes other makers' work in our words. No page is copied here. A number in brackets points at the source with that number in the register's own list.

Each register carries a date and a review-by date. `npm run check-skills` warns when one is overdue, and does not fail.

## Brand identity sheets
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

Six pages from two public brand manuals. Both are logo-led. There is no example of a colour-palette page with tints, and none of a type pairing page for a modern web brand.

### Layout
- A sheet is one idea. A small label at the top left, or a heading in the left margin, a short paragraph of rules beside it in one to three narrow columns, then the specimen alone in a large white field. A hairline rule separates heading from specimen (4, 5, 6). Page numbers sit small at the bottom right.
- A specimen never shares a page with a second topic, except to compare variants (5, 6).
- The common sheets: the mark on white with a rule paragraph (1, 4); a construction grid, the mark over a fine square grid with a note that gives the unit (2); a type specimen (3); a list of logo layouts, each with a bold name and one sentence of when to use it (5); colour formats and backgrounds (6).

### Imagery
The mark itself, large. The only photograph is one landscape used as a background test (6). No decoration, no mock-ups.

### Type
Manual text is small, one grotesque, regular for body and bold for the label (3, 4). The type sheet shows the alphabet in two weights, then numbers and punctuation, then paragraphs at two sizes and two column widths: the alphabet first, sample text second (3). Wordmarks are far larger than any text on the page.

### Colour
Almost none. Paper white, black text, one brand colour shown only on the sheet that names it: a single red on a white page (1), and navy plus red with print and screen values in two swatches (4). Colour variants are shown as full colour, black, and white knock-out on three backgrounds (6).

### Story
The sheet states a rule, then proves it: this is the colour, this is the grid, this is the face, these are the layouts, this is what not to do.

### CTA
A brand sheet has no button. Its call is a usage line: each variant carries one sentence of when to use it (5), and each colour carries its values so the next maker can apply it.
