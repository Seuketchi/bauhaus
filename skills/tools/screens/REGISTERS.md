# Screen registers

The look of a kind of screen, as a short list of rules read from real finished screens. Each register was written from pictures that were opened and looked at on the date given.

A register is a starting point, not a template. The brief names the register it used and what it departs from, and the piece's own research (`research.md`, step P3 in the `design` tool) adds what its sources show. A register ranks as an optional reference: the project's own rules, the brief and `DESIGN.md` outrank it, and a project's design gate decides over any of it.

These registers describe other makers' work in our words. No picture is copied here, and the work belongs to its makers. A number in brackets points at the source with that number in the register's own list.

Each register carries a date and a review-by date. `npm run check-skills` warns when one is overdue, and does not fail.

## Mobile app screens
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

Five of the six sources are store listing frames: a headline on a colour field with a phone, or a bare screen, below it. They show real app screens inside marketing frames. Only one is a plain in-app capture sequence. Fintech and banking come first, then commerce.

### Layout
- A portrait frame with the headline at the top, two to three lines taking 12 to 20 percent of the height, and a phone or screen block below it (1, 2, 5, 6).
- In-app money screens put a balance or total at the top, a card or hero under it, a list under that, and a bottom tab bar of 3 to 5 icons (1).
- A bare screen with no device can be the whole picture: a coloured keypad panel with the amount and its two actions on a grey ground (3).
- A capture-and-prefill flow is four phones side by side with a caption under each, showing one task from menu to filled form (4).
- Commerce home: a search bar, a promo banner, a round category row, a two-column product grid with price stickers, a tab bar (5, 6).

### Imagery
Almost none inside money screens: icons, merchant logos as round avatars, an emoji per savings goal (2). Commerce screens are the opposite: photo tiles in a grid, circular category thumbnails, product shots with price stickers (5, 6). Store frames add a device render or a held phone.

### Type
A geometric or grotesque sans, heavy for headlines on the store frames. In the app, the balance is the biggest type on the screen (2, 3). Row titles are medium, secondary text grey and small. Cents are set smaller than whole units (1).

### Colour
One brand colour owns the store frame and carries the identity: coral red (1), deep green (2), neon green on warm grey (3), orange (5), warm taupe with brown type (6). In-app screens go white or near-white with one accent for money in and the brand colour for the main card.

### Story
One claim per frame: a rate, a verb, an occasion, a catalogue. The first frame states the whole product and each later frame states one feature.

### CTA
Money screens pair two actions at the base of the amount, one filled and one outlined (3). A balance card carries chips for its actions (1). Commerce screens use a search bar and category circles as the first call, and a price sticker on the product (5, 6).

## Landing and product pages
Date: 2026-11-07
Review by: 2027-05-07

These are first-screen references, a little more than one screen each, from a gallery of other people's sites. The live sites may differ now. There are no dense documentation or pricing pages among them.

### Layout
- One column, centred or flush-left, over a full-width hero, then horizontal bands that alternate background, dark or tinted and then light (1, 4, 5).
- A thin nav bar floats on the hero, as a pill or card (1) or plain (2, 5). The hero is about one screen high. Product UI, when shown, sits at the bottom of the hero and is cropped by the fold (1, 4).
- Common shapes: a picture hero with the headline over it and a framed screenshot below (1, 4, 5); a product-as-hero with two equal rounded cards (2); a shop front with a manifesto line and a long list of category words beside a portrait (3); alternating bands with a three-column feature trio (4, 5).

### Imagery
The hero is a single large picture, not a collage: a painted landscape with a figure (1), a macro product photo (2), a packshot on crumpled black paper (3), a stylised mid-century illustration (5), a blueprint scene (4). Product UI is a framed screenshot, not a mock device. People are cropped close and smiling (3).

### Type
One display face at 56 to 90 px for a two-to-three-line headline, a small sans for the one-sentence sub. Accent by contrast of style inside the headline: an italic serif word (2), a thin weight against a heavy one (3), a condensed serif (5). Body copy stays under 16 px.

### Colour
One strong hero field or photo, then off-white or cream bands (4, 5). One accent only, for buttons and links. Buttons are pills or soft rectangles, one filled and one outlined.

### Story
The hero states the category change or the outcome in one line. The second band names the problem or the set of products. The third shows the product, usually as a tabbed or card list.

### CTA
Pills or soft rectangles, one filled and one outlined, in the page's single accent: a blue accent (4), a black pill (1, 3). The first is in the hero and the same action repeats under the product band.
